# Shared month-first parser swaps dates for day-first tenants — 379 reports

## Problem and assignment

Reports in #4471 describe day/month swaps: R-41200 (Harbour Dental, app, en-GB) reports 7 April becoming 4 July; R-41203 (Lantern Row, app, cs-CZ) reports 2 December becoming 12 February. The exported KB describes a shared parser that reads dates month first, conflicting with these tenants' day-first dates.

The executable assignment in `src/lib/kb/causes.ts:40` is precise: the tenant must exist and `dayFirst(tenant.locale)` must be true; the report belongs here unless its channel is `form` and its tenant's form prints a month-first hint. Thus app/email reports, including those from booking-v3 tenants, and booking-v2 form reports belong to `parser`. Expected behavior is that date interpretation agrees with the tenant's date order.

## Evidence and scope

`delivery/analysis.json`, generated by `node scripts/analyze.mjs --write` from the original classifier and exported data, assigns **379 unique reports across 11 tenants** to this group. Its received-date range is **2026-04-01–2026-10-04**. The full ID set is in the `parser` group's `ids` array.

The export contains 427 rows, 412 unique IDs and 15 repeated rows. After deduplication, the 379 parser IDs and 33 hint IDs are disjoint and total 412; none are unplaced. This issue covers the parser group only. Subject, browser locale and intake tag do not determine assignment: 23 of these reports carry an intake `hint` tag.

Dates of receipt and merging measure different events. Across #4471, **184 reports were merged on 2026-07-02**, which does not mean 184 arrived that day. For example, R-41200 was received on 2026-04-01 and merged on 2026-07-02. The accepted historical subject-based deduplication decision remains a record of desk policy; matching subjects do not establish a shared cause under the classifier.

## Footnote and limits

`src/lib/data/desk-notes.json` flags **R-41202, R-41209, R-41236, R-41344 and R-41620**. Their bodies describe unusual symptoms, including one-day differences, a June-only shift and an invite arriving before booking. All five remain counted once within the 379 parser reports, as `readAs` in `src/lib/kb/causes.ts:95` confirms. Their wording does not establish a timezone defect or a third cause; explaining those symptoms requires further evidence.

This finding describes the exported KB's classification and reported symptoms. The export does not include the production parser implementation or a verified fix.
